Plenty of Wasaga Beach cottages earn their keep in the weeks the family is not using them. Since early 2026 that has a rulebook of its own. Wasaga Beach Council passed By-law 2026-08 to license short-term accommodations, and a rental cottage now needs a licence that comes with drawings, inspections and an annual renewal. Ask any cottage builder Wasaga Beach owners are talking to how the building will be used, because the answer changes what goes on the site plan.

This page is about that overlap: what the licence asks for, which parts of it are really design decisions, and how we would plan a Wasaga Beach cottage that works for a family in July and for guests the rest of the season.

When a Wasaga Beach Cottage Becomes a Licensed Rental

The by-law defines a short-term accommodation as a dwelling, or part of one, rented for 30 consecutive days or less in a single stay. Rental cottages and rental cabins fall into licence Class C. Without a licence it is an offence to operate one, and also to market one: every listing and advertisement has to show the licence number the Town issues.

Operators who already held a licence had to reapply by April 1, 2026. Every licence, old or new, now expires on April 1 each year. It is not transferable from one person to another or from one property to another, and it becomes void the moment the cottage changes hands. If you are building a cottage partly as an investment, that last point matters for resale: a buyer starts the application over.

A cottage used only by your own family is not a rental, and the by-law is about renting. It is the first paid short stay, or the first listing, that brings a Wasaga Beach cottage under it.

The Licence Asks for a Site Plan. That Is a Design Job.

Schedule A, added by By-law 2026-17, lists what a Class C application has to include. Alongside ownership, identification and proof of $5,000,000 in liability insurance that names short-term accommodation, the applicant files a Neighbourhood Partnership Plan. Three parts of it are drawings or depend on them:

  • A to-scale site plan of the property showing every structure, temporary or permanent, plus fences, fire pits and outdoor burning devices, pools, hot tubs and swim spas, decks, docks, outdoor barbecue and cooking areas, garbage areas and parking surfaces.
  • A parking management plan, drawn to scale, showing the size and location of every parking space guests will use. It has to comply with the Town’s zoning by-law.
  • A garbage management plan that references the site plan and states where waste is stored, in what containers, and the total capacity.

A fourth part, the noise and nuisance strategy, can name measures such as acoustic fencing. For a new build, all of these are cheaper to draw into the original site plan than to retrofit around a finished cottage. Where the hot tub sits relative to the neighbour’s bedroom window, whether two cars and a boat trailer fit without anyone parking on the road, where a week of garbage for a full house waits for pickup: those are siting decisions, and we make siting decisions for a living.

Inspections Before the First Guest

Schedule A says the inspections must be completed and passed before a new Wasaga Beach licence is issued, within 60 calendar days of a complete application. Three of them land on the building.

Building compliance

A new cottage built under a building permit, with its occupancy inspection closed, starts this step with the paperwork already in hand.

Zoning compliance

Checks the use and the parking against the Wasaga Beach zoning by-law, which is why the parking plan is drawn to its numbers from day one.

Fire safety

A fire safety inspection is required for a new licence. Bedroom count, exits and alarms are largely settled at design stage, long before an inspector arrives.

Schedule A also lists an arrears compliance check and a property standards inspection. If an applicant does not pass within the 60 days, or an extension, the application is no longer valid and the process starts again.

Designing a Cottage for Both Kinds of Week

Our cottage work in Wasaga Beach starts from the same place as anywhere else: how the family wants to use the water and the deck. A rental season adds a few questions to that brief, and they are all answerable on paper.

Outdoor rooms placed with neighbours in mind

Our Panoramics designs are built around window walls, open interiors and integrated sundecks. On a rental cottage, which way those sundecks face is also a noise question for the Neighbourhood Partnership Plan, so we site them with the side yards and the next lot in view, not only the view.

Parking that fits the full house

A cottage that sleeps two families brings two families’ cars. We lay the parking surface out against the zoning by-law early, so the drawing the Town wants is a copy of the site plan rather than a new exercise.

Four-season, or not

A cottage that rents in shoulder seasons, or that may become a retirement home, earns its insulation and heating. We build four-season cottages that never have to be closed, and lighter seasonal ones where that suits the plan and the budget.

A plan line made for cottage lots

Our Country Retreats line is drawn for the cottage lifestyle, with open-concept plans, many on narrow or smaller footprints, and walkout options for sloping lots. Every plan is a starting point we reshape around your lot.

Running It From Away: the Responsible Person Rule

Many people who build a cottage in Wasaga Beach live somewhere else. The by-law has a rule aimed squarely at them. The owner, or a responsible person the owner designates, has to respond to the Town within 30 minutes of a call or email, and must be able to attend the cottage in person within one hour if asked. Guests sign a Renters’ Code of Conduct, and the owner keeps a guest register for two years, including how many vehicles each group brings.

That is worth deciding before you build, not after. It can change what you build: a cottage with a lock-off owner’s room or storage, or one designed to be simple for a local manager to turn over between groups, is easier to run under these rules than one designed only around your own family.

On the water side, the Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority regulates development near the Georgian Bay shoreline under Ontario Regulation 41/24, and notes that owners bordering Wasaga Beach Provincial Park must apply to Ontario Parks for a work permit before shoreline works. Both belong on the approvals list for a lot near the water, and conservation authority and waterfront approvals are part of what our permits service handles.

Wasaga Beach Cottage and Rental Questions

Do I need a licence to rent my Wasaga Beach cottage?

If you rent it for stays of 30 consecutive days or less, yes. The Town of Wasaga Beach By-law 2026-08 treats that as a short-term accommodation, places rental cottages and cabins in licence Class C, and prohibits operating or even advertising one without a licence.

What inspections does a new short-term rental licence need?

Schedule A of the by-law lists a building compliance inspection, a zoning compliance inspection, an arrears compliance check, a fire safety inspection and a property standards inspection, to be completed and passed before a licence is issued. The applicant has 60 calendar days from a complete application to get the inspections and documents approved.

Does the rental licence transfer if I sell the cottage?

No. The by-law says a short-term accommodation licence is not transferable between people or locations and is void on a change of ownership. Every licence also expires on April 1 each year and has to be renewed.

I live in Toronto. Can I still rent out a Wasaga Beach cottage?

The by-law requires the owner or a designated responsible person to answer the Town within 30 minutes and to attend the property in person within one hour if asked. If you are not that close, you will need someone who is.

My lot backs onto the provincial park. Does that change anything?

It can. The Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority notes that owners bordering Wasaga Beach Provincial Park, including the beach, must apply for an Ontario Parks work permit before shoreline works, and the shoreline itself is within NVCA’s regulated area.

Do you build cottages in Wasaga Beach?

Yes. Our cottage service page names Wasaga Beach as the southern end of the cottage country we have built in for five decades. Our office and model home are in Innisfil, so we travel to your lot.
