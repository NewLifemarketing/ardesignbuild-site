Orillia Building Permits

Planning Signs Off Before Building Will Look

A new house in Orillia goes past two City desks before it is issued, and an inspector at fifteen stages after. The schedule lives in that sequence.

Orillia building permits for a new house are not one application. They are two, to two different parts of the City, followed by up to fifteen inspections that each have to be booked two business days ahead. Most of the waiting on an Orillia build is not the City reading drawings; it is one step not being ready when the next one is due.

We are A&R Design Build. We have handled planning applications, permits and inspections as part of our builds across Central Ontario since 1973, and Orillia is part of the region we serve from our base in Innisfil. Here is the City of Orillia process in the order it actually runs, from the documents the City itself publishes.

Does Your Orillia Project Need a Permit?

The City of Orillia’s own lists, side by side.

Needs a permit

New construction, additions and major renovations. Structural changes, including adding or removing walls. Garages, decks and sheds larger than 15 m² (162 sq ft). Plumbing and HVAC work. Installing or changing a septic system. Demolition.

Garages, ADUs and bunkies

Does not

Painting, flooring and non-structural repairs. Fences, except pool enclosures. Windows or doors replaced with the same size. Cabinets installed without changes to plumbing.

Two Desks: Planning First, Then Building

The City’s guide for a new dwelling, BD.F.413, opens with an instruction that shapes the whole timeline: a Zoning Certificate is required before you apply for a residential building permit. The certificate is a Planning document, applied for through the City’s public portal or by email to the planning office, and it is checked against the zoning before anyone in Building opens the drawings.

What the Zoning Certificate needs is short: the application form and a site plan showing the proposed building, any existing buildings, the setbacks to every property line, and the existing driveway, parking and landscaped areas, plus detailed elevations. Short does not mean quick to prepare. The site plan has to be right, because the setbacks on it are the ones the house will be built to.

Then the building permit. For a detached, semi-detached or townhouse dwelling, the City’s guide asks for:

  • The building permit application form, the owner’s authorization if someone else is applying, the Schedule 1 designer information form and the Energy Efficiency Design Summary. The application form and authorization are not needed when you apply through the public portal.
  • Detailed floor plans, elevations, cross sections and details.
  • A lot grading plan. The City’s lot grading page accepts one prepared and stamped by a professional engineer, a certified engineering technologist or an Ontario land surveyor.
  • A heat loss and heat gain summary, an engineered truss design and layout, and an engineered floor system layout including any engineered beams, where they apply.

The guide adds one line for low-lying land: properties in the City’s Flood Hazard Overlay Zone must incorporate flood-proofing in line with the Ontario Building Code, under the City’s Building in Flood Plains policy. If your Orillia lot is near the water, ask about the overlay before the drawings are started, not after.

Paying for an Orillia Permit

The permit is not issued until the money side is settled, and for a new house the permit fee is the small part. BD.F.413 lists the typical charges together: building permit fees, City development charges, education development charges for the Simcoe County District School Board and the Simcoe Muskoka Catholic District School Board, and a lot grading deposit of $2,000.

The City’s development charge pamphlet says its charge is payable on the date the building permit is issued; from July 1, 2026 it is $31,771.58 for a single detached dwelling. The lot grading deposit breaks down as a $350 review fee that stays with the City and $1,650 that comes back once the grading is certified. Fees can be paid by cash, cheque, debit or credit card, with credit cards accepted online only and a 2.4% convenience fee on top.

Fifteen Inspections and the 48-Hour Rule

Once it is issued, the permit is worked off on site. The City of Orillia lists fifteen inspection stages and requires that each one is booked before construction moves to the next phase.

The booking rule is the part that sets the pace. Every inspection needs at least 48 hours’ notice, and the count starts on the following business day, not counting weekends, holidays or office closures. A request made on a Friday afternoon is a request for Wednesday. The City asks for the permit number, the address, the type of inspection, a date and time preference, and the builder’s or superintendent’s contact details.

Miss the mark and it costs. When work is incomplete or needs corrections, the City charges a re-inspection fee with a minimum of $171.78. Across fifteen stages, a builder who books inspections for work that is not ready can add both money and days to an Orillia build.

The last two stages matter most to the owner. The City says plainly that no occupancy is permitted until an Occupancy Permit is issued. Plan the move, the mortgage draw and the sale of your current house around that inspection, not around the day the painters leave.

How We Carry Orillia Building Permits

Planning and permit approvals are step five of our twelve-step process. On an Orillia house, it runs like this.

Confirm the municipality and the zoning

We check that the lot is inside the City of Orillia, what the zoning allows, and whether the Flood Hazard Overlay Zone touches it, before the design is fixed.

Zoning Certificate

We prepare the site plan and elevations and apply to Planning, so the setbacks are approved before the permit drawings are finished.

Permit application

Drawings, the stamped grading plan, the energy summary and the engineered layouts go to Building together, with your authorization for us to apply.

Inspections to occupancy

Inspections are booked to the construction schedule as each stage is ready, through to the occupancy permit and the final walk-through with you.

Orillia Permit Questions

Do I need a Zoning Certificate before an Orillia building permit?

Yes, for residential work. The City’s new-dwelling guide says a Zoning Certificate is required before you apply for a residential building permit. It is applied for through the Planning side of the City, with a site plan and detailed elevations.

How do I book an inspection in Orillia?

Through the City’s e-Permit portal or with the inspections staff, at least 48 hours in advance, counted from the next business day. Have the permit number, the address, the type of inspection, your preferred date and time, and the builder’s contact details ready.

What happens if the work is not ready when the inspector arrives?

The City charges a re-inspection fee, with a minimum of $171.78, when work is incomplete or corrections are needed.

Does a shed or deck need a permit in Orillia?

Garages, decks and sheds larger than 15 square metres (162 square feet) need one. Fences generally do not, except pool enclosures. Painting, flooring, non-structural repairs and same-size window or door replacements are also exempt.

Can I move into a new house in Orillia before the final inspection?

Not before the City issues an occupancy permit. The City is clear that no occupancy is permitted until it does. Occupancy is the 14th of the 15 inspection stages, with the final inspection after it.

What does the City charge besides the permit fee?

For a new dwelling the City lists building permit fees, City development charges, education development charges for both school boards, and a $2,000 lot grading deposit, of which $1,650 is refunded after the grading is certified. The development charge is payable when the permit is issued.
