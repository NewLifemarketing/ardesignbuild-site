Barrie Building Permit

The Clock Starts When the File Is Whole

Ontario gives the City ten days on a complete house application. Everything that decides your wait happens before that, and most of it is not on the City’s desk.

A Barrie building permit for a new house can come back in ten days. It can also sit for months without anyone at City Hall doing anything wrong. The difference is almost never the review. It is whether the file was complete when it went in, and in Barrie “complete” means approvals from offices that are not the building department at all.

We are a design-build firm based in Innisfil, and preparing permit drawings and applications is part of how we build homes, garages, ADUs and bunkies across Simcoe County. City of Barrie building permits follow the same Ontario Building Code as everywhere else in the province, but the file Barrie asks for has its own shape. This page is what we would tell a Barrie client before we file: how the clock works, what the City asks for, what the fees look like in 2026, and the shorter rules for decks, pools and fences that most people are actually searching for.

The Ten-Day Clock and the Condition Attached to It

The Province of Ontario’s guide to building permits is clear about the timeline: the Building Code requires a municipality to review a complete permit application within a set timeframe, and for a house that timeframe is 10 days. Within it, the City must issue the permit or refuse it with reasons.

The City of Barrie’s Applicable Law Checklist supplies the condition. It says no timeframe for building permit review can be established until all required applicable law approvals are completed and the approval documents are submitted to Building Services. It goes further: Building Services is required by law to prioritize applications that are fully complete. A file missing one approval does not wait in line. It waits outside it.

That is why we treat the permit as the last step of a sequence rather than the first. By the time a City of Barrie permit application goes into APLI, every question on the checklist should already have an answer on paper.

The Questions Only a Barrie Lot Gets Asked

Most applicable law questions are the same anywhere in Ontario. A few on Barrie’s checklist are there because of where the city is.

Two conservation authorities. The checklist names both the Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority and the Lake Simcoe Region Conservation Authority. If a lot abuts a ravine, watercourse, wetland or shoreline, development may need a construction and fill permit from the authority before any building permit can be issued, and the City notes that NVCA or LSRCA will confirm which one has jurisdiction.

Highway 400. The checklist names the highway outright. It asks whether a property is within 45 metres of the Highway 400 corridor limits, or within 395 metres of the centrepoint of an interchange. A yes brings the Ministry of Transportation into the file with its own building and land use permit.

Source water. Under the Clean Water Act, a property inside a Source Water Protection regulated area needs a section 59 screening form.

Heritage and history. Barrie keeps a municipal heritage register, and the checklist asks whether a property is listed, designated or inside a heritage district or study area. It also asks whether the site is a former waste disposal site or carries a Certificate of Property Use.

For most residential lots in established Barrie neighbourhoods every answer is no, and the checklist takes ten minutes. The lots where one answer is yes are often the ones bought for a ravine view or a quiet street near the highway, which is a good reason to run the checklist before you buy rather than after. A building permit Barrie can issue in ten days starts with a lot that has already cleared that list.

City of Barrie Building Permit Requirements for a New House

For new Part 9 houses, including a secondary dwelling or coach house, Barrie publishes a New Residential Dwelling Unit checklist dated September 2025. Read in order, Barrie’s list is a sequence rather than a pile.

Pre-approvals

A municipal address from Development Services, any executed subdivision agreement and, where site plan control applies, conditions signed off by Fire, Engineering, Water, Planning and Building. The City’s checklist notes that site plan approval is required for most new buildings other than houses and accessory structures, so a single house usually skips it.

Forms

The online APLI application, a Schedule 1 designer information form or drawings sealed by an architect or engineer, the Applicable Law Checklist, the Energy Efficiency Design Summary signed by the designers, and an owner consent letter when someone else applies for you.

Site plan and grades

Property lines, setbacks, the footprint, water and sanitary services, existing and proposed grades and swales, sump pump discharge, and the underside of footing, top of foundation and finished floor elevations.

Architectural and structural drawings

Floor plans, elevations and sections, window and door sizes, spatial separation calculations, stairs and guards, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and engineered roof truss and floor joist drawings.

Payments before issuance

Development charges, parkland payment and any outstanding permit fees are paid before the permit is issued, not at application.

Two items on that list deserve a word. The site plan’s grade information is what lets the City see where water will go, and on an infill lot it is also what a neighbour’s basement depends on. And the designer form is not a formality: Schedule 1 identifies the individual who takes responsibility for each design activity under the Building Code.

Barrie Building Permit Fees in 2026

Barrie sets its fees in Schedule K of the Fees By-law. City of Barrie building permit fees for a new house are calculated per square metre of total floor area, basement included, and then the extras that apply are added. The 2026 figures that matter for a house and its outdoor projects:

A worked example, using an invented house rather than a real one: 180 m² above grade over a 120 m² basement is 300 m² of floor area. At $17.38 that is $5,214, plus the $20 pre-screening fee. If the lot is an infill lot outside site plan or subdivision review, the $579.43 grading review is added. Development charges are a different and much larger line, and Barrie collects them before the permit is issued.

Two fees in the schedule are worth avoiding rather than budgeting for. Starting work before a permit is issued adds a surcharge, and a stop work order on a house adds 25 percent of the permit fee, with a $500 minimum in 2026. A failed inspection that has to be repeated costs $196.34 each time. A complete file and a site that is ready when the inspector arrives are cheaper than either.

Deck, Pool and Fence: The Shorter Tracks

Plenty of people looking for building permits Barrie issues are not building a house. Here is what the City’s own pages and by-laws say about the three projects people ask about most. We include them because they come up on our own builds, not because we take them on as standalone jobs.

Decks

A deck attached to a building is on the City’s list of work needing a permit, so a deck permit Barrie issues is the norm for anything off the back door. The City of Barrie deck permit fee in 2026 is $208.08 without a roof and $310.00 with one. Zoning By-law section 5.3.5.2 keeps decks 0.6 metres from rear and interior side lot lines, 1.5 metres from an exterior side lot line and 3 metres from the front lot line, and a deck less than 1.8 metres above grade does not count toward lot coverage. On a new Barrie house we build, the deck details travel with the house drawings.

Pools

The Barrie pool permit is really a permit for the enclosure, and timing is the part that surprises people. Pool Enclosure By-law 2024-094 says no one may excavate for, erect or install a pool until the pool enclosure permit has been issued. The enclosure must be at least 1.22 metres high, leave no gap that a 102 mm sphere could pass through, and have gates with self-closing hinges and self-latching hardware. The City of Barrie pool permit costs $307.89 in 2026 and is applied for through APLI. An in-ground installation also appears on the City’s list of work needing a Right-Of-Way Activity permit, so a pool permit Barrie issues is not always the only one.

Fences

There is no City of Barrie fence permit for an ordinary privacy fence: the City lists privacy fences among projects that do not need a building permit. The zoning by-law still applies. Section 5.3.5.4 limits a fence in a residential front yard to 1 metre above grade, and a fence over 2 metres is treated as an accessory structure with all the rules that come with one. A fence around a pool is a different matter, and falls under the pool enclosure permit above.

The Barrie Permit Portal, From Application to Final

Every one of these Barrie applications runs through APLI, the Barrie permit portal. It needs a free registered account. Once you are in, an application can be started, saved and finished later, fees up to $10,000 can be paid online, and the reviewed drawings and permit placard are posted there when they are ready.

City of Barrie building permit status is visible in real time, with email notices as the file moves. Inspections are requested in the same place, and Ontario’s guide notes that the building official must inspect within two working days of being notified. After each visit the City emails the result to the applicant, the agent, the prime consultant and the site supervisor, marked passed, failed or in progress. New buildings and additions are inspected before anyone moves in.

When we file for a client, the client is the owner and we are the agent, so both of us see every result. Laura McAdam leads design, planning applications and municipal approvals on our team, and the drawings, forms and correspondence come from us. You get the outcome without having to learn the portal, though you are always welcome to log in and watch it. If an old permit record is what you need, for a renovation or a sale, the City handles those as building permit record requests, charged at $46.24 per 15 minutes of file search in 2026.

Barrie Building Permit Questions

How long does a City of Barrie building permit take?

Ontario sets a 10 day timeframe for a municipality to decide on a complete permit application for a house. Barrie’s Applicable Law Checklist adds the condition that matters: no review timeframe can be established until every required approval from another agency is in and submitted. A complete file starts the clock; an incomplete one does not.

How much are Barrie building permit fees for a new house?

Under Schedule K of the City’s Fees By-law, a new Part 9 house is charged $17.38 per square metre in 2026, measured on total floor area including the basement, plus a $20 non-refundable pre-screening fee. A grading review of $579.43 applies to projects outside site plan, condominium or subdivision review. Development charges and parkland payments are separate and are due before the permit is issued.

Do I need a permit for a deck in Barrie?

The City lists decks attached to a building among projects that need a building permit. Schedule K sets the 2026 fee at $208.08 for a deck or porch without a roof and $310.00 with one. Zoning also applies: decks must stay at least 0.6 metres from rear and interior side lot lines, 1.5 metres from an exterior side lot line and 3 metres from the front.

When do I need a pool permit in Barrie?

Before you dig. Barrie’s Pool Enclosure By-law 2024-094 says no one may excavate for, erect or install a pool until a pool enclosure permit has been issued. The application goes through APLI, and the 2026 fee is $307.89. An in-ground pool installation is also on the City’s list of work that needs a Right-Of-Way Activity permit.

How do I check my Barrie building permit status?

Log in to APLI, the City’s online portal. It gives real-time status updates and email notifications, shows reviewed drawings and the permit placard, and is where inspections are requested. After each inspection, the City emails the result to the applicant, agent, prime consultant and site supervisor.

Will A&R handle the Barrie permit for my new home?

Yes, for a home, garage, ADU or bunkie we are building. Preparing the permit drawings and applications and managing the back-and-forth with the municipality is part of our design-build service, led on our team by Laura McAdam. We do not advertise standalone deck, pool or fence permits.
