Before, During and After

Twelve Steps, From First Conversation to Keys in Hand

Building a custom home is one of the biggest projects of your life. It shouldn’t feel like a leap of faith. Here’s exactly how we take you from first conversation to keys in hand — and beyond.

Pre-Consultation

We start by listening. We learn about your vision, needs, lifestyle, budget and timeline.

Site Visit & Evaluation

We visit your property to assess conditions and determine the best location for your new home or cottage.

Custom Design

Bring us your ideas, or let us design a custom home that reflects your lifestyle and fits your lot and budget.

Preliminary Plans & 3D Renderings

We create preliminary drawings, elevations and 3D renderings so you can see your future home come to life.

Planning & Permit Approvals

We assist with planning applications and handle all the permits and inspections, so the process is smooth and stress-free.

Exterior Selections

Choose your exterior finishes with expert guidance in our Design Centre — siding, stone, roofing, windows, doors, trim, decking and more.

Interior Selections

Work with our interior design team to select cabinets, countertops, flooring, lighting, plumbing, millwork and all the finishes that make it yours.

Site Preparation

Lot clearing, blasting, excavation, septic, well, driveway and foundation preparation.

Construction

Our skilled team and trusted trades bring your home to life with careful planning, quality workmanship and consistent communication.

Framing Walk-through

We walk through your home together before drywall to review every detail and confirm layouts, electrical, plumbing, HVAC and upgrades.

Final Walk-through & Possession

We complete a final inspection, review all features, sign the Tarion warranty forms and hand over the keys to your new home or cottage.

Beyond Move-In

We’re here for you after you move in. Every build comes complete with a 7-year Tarion structural warranty, manufacturer’s warranties and our 1-year builder warranty — for complete peace of mind.

One Team, One Set of Drawings

The seams in a construction project are where time and money leak: between the designer and the builder, between the site contractor and the foundation crew, between whoever drew it and whoever has to build it. Each handover is an opportunity for something to be assumed rather than confirmed.

Running design, approvals, site work and construction inside one company removes those seams rather than managing them. It also means there is one set of drawings everybody is working from, and one phone number when something needs a decision.

Decisions in the Order That Costs Least

Every project has the same decisions in it; what varies is when they are made. Made at the drawing stage they cost a revision. Made once material is on site they cost material, labour and schedule, and sometimes a permit amendment on top.

So the sequence is deliberate: the lot is assessed before the design is finalised, the design is finished before the permit is filed, and the permit is in hand before ground is broken. Each stage closes before the next one opens, which is slower at the front and considerably faster overall.

What Happens on Site

Site preparation comes first and sets everything that follows — clearing, excavation, the driveway, grading, and the well and septic where servicing is private. Then the foundation, the framing, the envelope, and the systems inside it.

Inspections punctuate all of it at defined stages, each called and passed before the work is covered up. The finishing work comes last and is where the build packages differ; everything before it is built to the same standard regardless of which package was chosen.

Communication During the Build

Most frustration on a construction project traces back to not knowing what is happening rather than to what is happening. A week with no visible progress on site is often a week waiting on an inspection or a delivery, and knowing which makes it tolerable.

So the expectation is set at the start: who to call, how often there is an update, and what happens when a decision is needed quickly. Decisions requested with no notice are the ones people regret, and they are avoidable by flagging them before they are urgent.

Where Clients Have the Most Influence

At the drawing stage, by a wide margin. That is where layout, orientation, size and the relationship between rooms are decided, and where changing any of them costs a revision rather than a rebuild.

Later, influence shifts to selections — finishes, fixtures, colours — which matter to how a house feels but not to how it works. Clients who spend their energy early and their patience late tend to be the happiest with the result.

Why the Order Rarely Changes

People occasionally ask whether site work can start while drawings are finished, to save a few weeks. Sometimes a small amount can, but mostly it cannot, because where the building sits is not settled until the drawings are and the ground has been tested.

Clearing and excavating to the wrong footprint is the most expensive kind of early start there is. The weeks it appears to save are generally lost twice over putting it right.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the stages of an A&R build?

Design and plan selection, then permits and township approvals, then site preparation, then construction, then handover and the warranty period that follows. One team runs all of it rather than handing the job between separate trades.

How long does the whole process take?

The approvals often take as long as the construction, which surprises people. Township review, conservation authority sign-off where shoreline work is involved and the building permit all sit ahead of the build, which is why that work starts early.

Who is my point of contact?

One accountable team runs the project from the first sketch through to after-care. The handover points between design, permitting, site work and construction are where projects usually lose time, and keeping them inside one company is what removes them.

Can I make changes once building has started?

Yes, but they cost more the later they happen. A change on a drawing is a revision; the same change once framing is standing means demolition, materials and schedule. That is why the design stage is deliberately front-loaded.

Do you build on my lot or do you supply land?

On your lot. If you have not bought one yet we can look at a property with you first and tell you what it will and will not carry, because the land shapes the build more than any other single factor.

What happens after the build is finished?

Handover and the warranty period. A&R Design Build is licensed by the Home Construction Regulatory Authority, the Ontario body that licenses new home builders and vendors.
