Garages, ADUs & Bunkies

More Room for the Life You’re Building

A Muskoka bunkie is the outbuilding most cottage families ask about first — somewhere the grandkids can stay all summer without anybody giving up the couch. Close behind it come the garage for the boat and the snowmobiles, and the accessory dwelling unit for family or rental income.

We design and build them with the same care as our homes — matched to your main building, properly permitted, and built to last Central Ontario winters.

  • Custom garages — attached, detached, and workshop-sized
  • Bunkies and sleeping cabins for overflow cottage guests
  • Accessory dwelling units (ADUs) and in-law suites
  • Full permit handling, including zoning for secondary structures

What a Muskoka Bunkie Has to Do

A bunkie earns its keep two weekends a year and then sits through eight months of snow, so the question is never only how it looks in July. Muskoka bunkies are built on the same footings, with the same envelope detailing, as the cottage they sit beside — because the one that was framed like a shed is the one with ice on the inside of the windows by February.

The sizes people ask for cluster tightly. A 10x16 bunkie is the most common footprint in cottage country, and it is no accident: at 160 square feet it sleeps four without triggering the permit threshold that a larger building does. Searches for 10 x 16 bunkie plans and 10x16 bunkie plans run all year, and the reason is that one number does a lot of work.

Building a Muskoka Bunkie, and When a Permit Applies

Under the Ontario Building Code a detached accessory structure needs a building permit once it exceeds 10 square metres — about 108 square feet. A 10x16 bunkie is 160 square feet, so it needs one. A bunkie with a bathroom needs one at any size, because plumbing is regulated separately from floor area.

Zoning is the other half and it is the half that surprises people. Setbacks from the water, lot coverage and whether a second sleeping building is permitted at all are decided by the township, not the Building Code, and they differ between Muskoka Lakes, Bracebridge, Gravenhurst and Huntsville. We handle both halves — see planning, permits and township approvals.

Custom Garages in Muskoka

A custom garage in Muskoka is usually sized around what has to fit rather than around a standard plan: a 24x30 garage takes two vehicles and a workbench, a 24 x 20 garage plan suits a single bay with storage, and a 30x30 garage plan is where the boat, the trailer and the sled all go indoors.

Garage plans with loft are the ones we are asked for most, and detached residential garage plans with a finished room above do two jobs on one footprint — storage below, guest space or a workshop over it. Plans for a two car garage with apartment take that further and start to overlap with what Ontario now calls an accessory dwelling unit.

Accessory Dwelling Units and In-Law Suites

Ontario changed what is possible here. The More Homes Built Faster Act, 2022 allows up to three residential units as-of-right on most serviced residential lots in the province, which is why accessory dwelling unit builders are busier than they were and why accessory dwelling unit construction cost is a question we now get on first calls rather than second ones.

What an accessory dwelling unit in Ontario costs depends far more on servicing than on finish: whether water and septic can carry another unit, whether the hydro service needs upgrading, and whether the lot can take the structure where you want it. Those three answers move the number more than any choice of cabinetry.

Muskoka Bunkie Plans, or a Drawing of Your Own

Some people arrive with bunkie plans already printed and some arrive with a photograph. Either works. Our home plans are a starting point rather than a catalogue order, and the same is true of an outbuilding — a 10x16 bunkie can gain a porch, lose a wall to a window, or turn ninety degrees to face the water.

What does not change is the part nobody photographs: the footing, the flashing, the envelope and the permit. Those are what make a Muskoka bunkie a building rather than a shed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a building permit for a bunkie in Muskoka?

Under the Ontario Building Code a detached accessory structure needs a permit once it is larger than 10 square metres, roughly 108 square feet. A 10x16 bunkie is 160 square feet, so it does. Any bunkie with plumbing needs a permit regardless of size. Township zoning applies separately and can restrict where the building sits even when the Building Code would allow it.

How big can a bunkie be before it needs a permit?

10 square metres — about 108 square feet — is the Building Code threshold for a detached structure. That is roughly 10 feet by 10 feet. Anything larger, including the common 10x16 bunkie, requires a building permit. Lot coverage limits in the local zoning by-law may bite before the Building Code does.

Can a bunkie have a bathroom?

Yes, and it is one of the most common requests. A bunkie with a bathroom needs a building permit at any size because plumbing is regulated separately from floor area, and the septic system has to be assessed for the additional load. On an older system that assessment is the first thing to do, not the last.

How much does an accessory dwelling unit cost to build?

Servicing drives it more than finishes. Whether the existing well and septic can carry another unit, whether the hydro service needs upgrading, and where the structure can legally sit all move the figure more than any interior choice. We price those three first so the number you get is the real one.

Are accessory dwelling units allowed in Ontario?

The More Homes Built Faster Act, 2022 permits up to three residential units as-of-right on most serviced residential lots in Ontario. Rural and shoreline lots on private water and septic are treated differently, and the township still controls setbacks, parking and lot coverage, so the as-of-right rule is the start of the conversation rather than the end of it.

What size garage should I build?

Work from what has to fit rather than from a standard size. A 24 x 20 garage suits one vehicle with storage, a 24x30 garage takes two and a workbench, and a 30x30 garage is where a boat or trailer joins them. If you want a finished room above, garage plans with loft change the foundation and framing from the outset, so decide that before drawings rather than after.
