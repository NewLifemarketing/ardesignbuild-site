Can a Bungalow Have a Basement?

Yes, and It Is Usually Worth Doing

Can a bungalow have a basement? Yes. A bungalow is one storey above the ground, and nothing about that stops a full floor underneath it.

The word describes what the house looks like from the road, not what it stands on. A great many bungalows have a full basement, some have a walkout, some are raised so the lower floor sits half out of the ground, and some sit on a slab with nothing below at all. Which one suits you depends far more on the lot and on how you plan to live than on the style of the house.

Excavation and foundations are the part of the build I look after, so this is the way I would talk it through with you before anything is drawn: why most of the digging happens whether you want a basement or not, what a basement under a one-storey house actually gives you, and what the ground has to say about it.

Can a bungalow have a basement? The short answer is yes

A bungalow is a house with all of its main living on one level above the ground. That is the whole definition. It says nothing about what is below the main floor, and there is no rule in the building code or in the trade that says a one-storey house cannot have a storey beneath it.

So the useful question is not whether a bungalow can have a basement. It is whether yours should, and what kind. That depends on three things: how deep the foundation has to go regardless, what the lot is made of and how it slopes, and what you want the lower floor to do for you.

You are digging most of the way down anyway

In a cold climate a house cannot sit on the surface. Ground that freezes expands, and a footing resting in it gets lifted and dropped every winter. So Part 9 of the National Building Code, the part written for houses, which provincial codes are built on, sets a minimum depth for every foundation in Table 9.12.2.2.

The table turns on two things: the soil, and whether the space inside the foundation is heated. On clay, or on soil that is not clearly identified, a foundation has to go at least 1.2 metres down. If there is no heated space inside it, it has to go below the depth of frost penetration as well. On rock there is no minimum, and on well-drained sand and gravel there is no minimum for a foundation around heated space.

On most lots, then, a bungalow on a crawl space or a frost wall already pays for a trench well below grade, for footings, and for concrete walls tall enough to reach them. A full basement asks for the rest of the depth and a slab at the bottom. It is more digging and more wall, but it is not a different kind of job. That is why so many bungalows have a basement, and why it is worth pricing one before deciding against it. Our page on how deep a frost wall goes covers the shallow alternative in more detail.

A bungalow basement is as big as the house

Here is the part that makes the question matter more for a bungalow than for any other house. A basement is the size of the footprint, and a bungalow puts everything on one floor, so its footprint is large. On a two-storey house of the same living area, the basement is roughly half the size of the house. On a bungalow it is the size of the whole house.

That cuts both ways. It is a lot of floor: room for guests, a workshop, storage, mechanical equipment, a games room. It is also a lot of excavation, a lot of foundation wall and a lot of slab. The footprint is also, as our plan pages put it, "the number that matters for setbacks and lot coverage". A bungalow that has to grow will often grow down rather than out, because going down does not take any more of the lot.

Our home plans list living area and footprint separately for exactly this reason. A plan with a large living area on a modest footprint is getting there with a second storey or a walkout level, and that changes what a given lot will accept.

Full basement, walkout, raised, or none

There are four common answers, and they suit different ground.

  • Full basement. The lower floor sits entirely below grade, with small windows near the top of the wall. It suits a flat lot with soil that drains and digs easily.
  • Walkout. On a lot that falls away, one side of the lower floor is underground and the other opens at ground level with full windows and a door.
  • Raised bungalow. The main floor is lifted so the lower level sits partly above the ground, with larger windows than a full basement allows. The Bayview, a raised bungalow we built, has a slab-on-grade walkout.
  • No basement. The house sits on a slab or a shallow foundation, and everything lives on one level. Our page on slab on grade explains what goes under the concrete and when it fits.

When can a bungalow have a basement with a walkout?

When the lot slopes. The grade is what creates the walkout, and on our walkout basement plans page we put it plainly: on flatter ground the same effect can sometimes be engineered with excavation and retaining, but that adds cost that a naturally sloping lot gives you for free. Where the slope is there, a walkout turns the fall of the land into a second full storey with windows and a door at grade. The excavation and the foundation are happening regardless, which is why we call it the cheapest square footage on any build.

Can a bungalow have a basement on any lot? What the ground decides

Not on every lot, or not at the same price. Three things in the ground change the answer.

Rock. The code sets no minimum depth for a foundation on rock, because rock does not heave. That is good news for a slab or a shallow foundation and bad news for a basement, because getting a full storey down into rock means breaking it. Blasting is one of the site preparation items we list on how we build, and on a rocky lot it can decide the whole question.

Water. A basement is a concrete box sitting in wet ground. A high water table or a low spot that collects runoff does not rule one out, but it changes the drainage, the waterproofing and sometimes the depth. The code also asks that backfill be graded so water runs away from the foundation after it settles, which is easier on some lots than others.

Slope and servicing. A slope is an argument for a walkout. On a lot with a septic system, the depth of the lowest drain matters too: a basement bathroom below the line that runs to the tank needs a pump to lift waste up to it, and the septic design has to know about it from the start. Our page on building on a sloped lot goes further into how grade changes a foundation.

We assess the lot before the design is finished for this reason. Can a bungalow have a basement on your particular piece of ground? That is a question the ground answers, and it is far cheaper to ask it early.

Living on one floor, with bedrooms below

People choose a bungalow to live on one level, often with the years ahead in mind. We build bungalows and accessible designs for aging in place, and the usual arrangement is to keep everything you use every day on the main floor and give the basement the things you use some of the time: guest rooms, a hobby space, storage, the mechanical room.

If a bedroom goes downstairs, the code has something specific to say. Under Article 9.9.10.1, a floor with a bedroom on it needs a way out other than the stairs: a door to the outside, or a window that opens from the inside without tools, with an unobstructed opening of at least 0.35 square metres and no dimension under 380 millimetres. The window also has to stay open on its own. In a full basement that usually means a larger window and a window well outside it; in a walkout or a raised bungalow, the bigger windows are often there already.

The stairs are the other thing to plan. On a one-storey house the stair to the basement takes space on the main floor, and where it lands decides how the lower floor works. That is a drawing question, and it is worth getting right before the foundation is laid out.

Dry and warm comes before finished

A basement people actually use is one that stays dry and does not feel cold. On our walkout plans page we describe what it takes: insulating the below-grade walls properly, treating the slab as part of the thermal envelope, and managing drainage around the foundation so the lower level stays dry. Done well, the space is used as heavily as the main floor. Done badly, it is storage.

Finishing it is a different matter, and it can wait. A basement can be left unfinished at first and completed later. As we say about build packages, deferring finishes is reversible, and deferring envelope quality is not, because getting back to it means opening up finished work. So the insulation under the slab, the drainage and the waterproofing are done right the first time, and the drywall and flooring downstairs can follow when the budget allows. Our page on below slab insulation covers the layer that has to go in before the concrete.

Can a bungalow have a basement added later? Decide it on paper

A basement is decided at the drawing stage or not at all. Adding one, or changing a full basement to a walkout, is a drawing change while it is on paper and a foundation change once concrete is ordered. As we put it in how we build, clearing and excavating to the wrong footprint is the most expensive kind of early start there is.

The excavation also has its own rules once it starts. The code asks that the footings sit on undisturbed soil and that the bottom of the excavation be kept from freezing for the whole construction period, which matters if the digging runs into late fall. None of that is unusual. It just means the basement is planned with the foundation, not added to it.

If you are weighing a bungalow with a basement against one without, bring us the lot. We will tell you what it will carry, and price the floor below before you decide against it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a bungalow have a basement on a flat lot?

Yes. A flat lot suits a full basement, with the whole lower floor below grade and windows near the top of the walls. What a flat lot does not give you is a walkout, because the slope is what creates one. A walkout can sometimes be engineered on flatter ground with excavation and retaining walls, but that adds cost a sloping lot gives you for free.

Is a basement worth it under a bungalow?

Often, because a foundation in a cold climate has to go well below grade regardless. On clay the code asks for at least 1.2 metres. A basement takes that same excavation and foundation the rest of the way down and gives you a floor the size of the whole house. Whether it is worth it on your lot depends on rock, water and slope, which is why we price it early.

Can you put a bedroom in a bungalow basement?

Yes, as long as the floor has a second way out. Under Article 9.9.10.1 of the code, that is a door to the outside or a window that opens from the inside without tools, with an unobstructed opening of at least 0.35 square metres and no dimension under 380 millimetres. In a full basement it usually means a larger window and a window well.

Can you add a basement to a bungalow later?

It is possible, by lifting the house or digging out underneath it, but it is far more work and disruption than digging before the house is built. That is why we settle the question while the plan is still a drawing. Changing your mind then is a drawing change. Changing it later is a foundation job under a finished house.

Does a bungalow basement have to be finished right away?

No. A basement can be left unfinished and completed later. What cannot wait is the part you cannot get back to: the drainage, the waterproofing, the insulation under the slab and on the walls. Get those right during the build and the finishes downstairs can follow when it suits you.
