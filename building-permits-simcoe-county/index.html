Approvals in Simcoe County, and Who Issues Them

Permits go through the County of Simcoe and its member municipalities. Requirements, fees and review times are set locally, so the first move on any new lot is confirming what applies at that address rather than carrying over the last project’s answer.

Whether a lot is on municipal services or a private well and septic system changes the path substantially. On-site sewage serving a single lot under 10,000 litres per day falls under Part 8 of the Ontario Building Code, which sets sizing and clearances — including a minimum 5 metre separation between any structure and a septic bed’s distribution pipes. On a constrained lot, that clearance decides where the building sits.

Simcoe County in Context

We work across Central Ontario, and Simcoe County sits alongside Barrie, Innisfil, Oro-Medonte in what we cover. The reason that matters is that the rules and the ground change as you move between them — the County of Simcoe and its member municipalities is not the same authority as the township next door, and the ground is frequently not the same ground.

Knowing where those lines fall is most of the value of working with someone who builds across the region rather than in one town.

What Happens First

The useful first step is not a quote. It is a conversation about the lot — what the County of Simcoe and its member municipalities will require, what the ground will carry, how it is serviced, and how you want to use the building. Those four answers set a realistic budget; a number produced before them is a guess wearing a decimal point.

If you have not bought the lot yet, that is the best time to talk. These constraints are far cheaper to evaluate before a purchase than after — including when the answer is that you should look at a different parcel.
